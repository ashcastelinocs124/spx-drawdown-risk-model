How stances were classified

Moratorium: called for pausing or stopping new data center construction. Make them pay: wants data centers to cover their own power, grid or water costs, or to lose tax breaks, without a pause. Local control / mixed: says communities should decide, or weighs benefits against costs. Supportive: promotes building them. No clear record: no sourced position found, which is not the same as no position.

AI policy: Stronger regulation means new rules, a regulator, testing mandates or opposing a ban on state AI laws; Light-touch means emphasizing build-out and competing with China over new rules; Mixed covers narrower or competing positions. Positions were read from public records and news; some rest on a single source or on aggregator summaries, and nothing was inferred from party.

Exposure: each company's announced US data center dollars (Jan 2024 – Nov 2026) are placed in the state where the site is and labeled with the data center stance of that state's 2026 Senate winner. Only dollar figures stated by the company or a state or local official, or in the company's own permit filings, are counted. Where a later figure replaces an earlier one, only the latest counts. Stargate sites are listed under OpenAI / Stargate rather than Oracle. "At least" and "more than" figures count at their stated floor. The list is a floor too: some announcements gave no dollar figure, and smaller ones may be missing. States with no race this year still have officeholders whose views aren't shown here.

Regulatory index: each state is scored on 20 indicators using a fixed rubric (allowed levels only, e.g. moratorium 0/25/60/100). A component is the average of its scored indicators, and the composite is the weighted sum: Power 30%, Permitting 25%, Tax 15%, Environment 15%, Operating rules 10%, Administration 5%. Where no rule was found after searching, the indicator scores as unrestricted with low confidence, so lower-scoring states partly reflect absence of evidence. Governor-elect pledges count only as "proposed" levels. Indicators with no source are left unscored.

Governors: winners and stances for all 36 races were classified the same way as senators. Several governor-elect stances are campaign pledges rather than enacted policy, and incumbents' records include signed bills and executive orders.
